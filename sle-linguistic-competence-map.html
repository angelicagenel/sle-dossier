What Students Learn: Six Skill Areas

Every SLE lesson develops six interconnected skill areas that together build real communicative ability. The first five organize language and context; Communication Strategies keep learners participating when their language is not sufficient yet.

Skill 1

Vocabulary

The words and phrases learners need, organized by real-life situations: not random lists. Each level builds a new set of high-frequency terms anchored in Mexican cultural contexts.

Skill 2

Grammar & Verb Patterns

How sentences work: from basic present tense to subjunctive and conditional. Includes verb combinations like ir a + infinitive and tener que + infinitive that express intention, obligation, and possibility.

Skill 3

Connectors & Text Organization

The words that hold ideas together: from simple sequencing (primero, después) to argumentative structure (sin embargo, por lo tanto). Without connectors, sentences are islands.

Skill 4

Pronunciation & Writing

How Spanish sounds and how it's written. Covers the vowel system, accent marks as grammar markers, spelling changes in verb forms, and intonation patterns that carry meaning.

Skill 5

Context, Culture & Appropriateness

Knowing what to say is grammar. Knowing how to say it is this. Covers register (tú/usted), courtesy, and context-specific interactional choices in the communities and situations represented.

Skill 6

Communication Strategies

What to do when you don't have the right word: repair, circumlocution, hedging, self-correction, and text editing. The skill that keeps communication alive while the others catch up.

SP101: Elementary Spanish I

Performance target: consistent Novice Mid, with guided extension toward Novice High
Five units, 24 regular lessons, and five cumulative Cuéntame capstones, built on a Mexico City-to-Veracruz narrative route.
Reading rule: cultural texts may contain supported receptive language, but student production and grading never exceed the grammar, chunks, and vocabulary already taught. The present progressive (gerund) enters productively only in the final lesson, L24.

Narrative and cultural route

Mexico City (CDMX) → Morelos (Tepoztlán–Cuernavaca) → Xochimilco → Michoacán/Estado de México → Veracruz/Córdoba. Each unit is grounded in a specific place, community, and source — the course does not treat one region's practices as representative of "Mexican culture" as a whole.

Five units

Unit 1

Hola, soy yo

Identity, register, spelling, existence, numbers 0–31. Closer: Huehuetéotl.

Unit 2

El mundo que te rodea

Description, tener, possession, relationships, spaces. Closer: Los alebrijes de Pedro Linares.

Unit 3

La vida en movimiento

Regular present-tense actions, questions, preferences, routine. Closer: El ajolote curioso / Xólotl.

Unit 4

Tiempo, números y agenda

Time, dates, numbers to 100, prices, weather, obligations. Closer: Las mariposas monarca.

Unit 5

Aquí y ahora

Location, destination, immediate plans, states, actions in progress. Closer: La Mulata de Córdoba.

For the complete lesson-by-lesson scope & sequence — communicative function, structures, vocabulary, and cultural anchor for every one of the 24 lessons — see the SP101 Course Outline (instructor resource).

SP102: Elementary Spanish II

ACTFL target: Intermediate Low
Past narration, reflexive verbs, and the preterite/imperfect contrast, building on SP101's communicative foundation. Introduces object pronouns, including direct object pronouns, for the first time.
Upcoming — proposed scope, pending final architecture review

SP102 is expected to grow learners from describing the present into narrating the past: telling stories, explaining habits and obligations, describing people and experiences in detail, and sustaining longer conversations about daily life, health, and celebrations. The specific unit breakdown and can-do statements are still being finalized and are not published here until approved.

SP201: Intermediate Spanish I

ACTFL target: Intermediate Mid/High
Commands, subjunctive, future, conditional, and argumentative discourse, moving students into confident, nuanced expression.
Upcoming — proposed scope, pending final architecture review

SP201 is expected to grow learners from narration into subjectivity and argument: giving instructions, expressing feelings and doubt with the subjunctive, projecting plans and hypotheses, reflecting on experience, and taking a supported position on a topic. The specific unit breakdown and can-do statements are still being finalized and are not published here until approved.
