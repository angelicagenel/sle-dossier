What Students Learn: Six Skill Areas

Every SLE lesson develops six interconnected skill areas that together build real communicative ability. The first five organize language and context; Communication Strategies keep learners participating when their language is not sufficient yet.

Skill 1

Vocabulary

The words and phrases learners need, organized by real-life situations: not random lists. Each level builds a new set of high-frequency terms anchored in Mexican cultural contexts.

Skill 2

Grammar & Verb Patterns

How sentences work: from basic present tense to subjunctive and conditional. Includes verb combinations like ir a + infinitive and tener que + infinitive that express intention, obligation, and possibility.

Skill 3

Connectors & Text Organization

The words that hold ideas together: from simple sequencing (primero, después) to argumentative structure (sin embargo, por lo tanto). Without connectors, sentences are islands.

Skill 4

Pronunciation & Writing

How Spanish sounds and how it's written. Covers the vowel system, accent marks as grammar markers, spelling changes in verb forms, and intonation patterns that carry meaning.

Skill 5

Context, Culture & Appropriateness

Knowing what to say is grammar. Knowing how to say it is this. Covers register (tú/usted), courtesy, and context-specific interactional choices in the communities and situations represented.

Skill 6

Communication Strategies

What to do when you don't have the right word: repair, circumlocution, hedging, self-correction, and text editing. The skill that keeps communication alive while the others catch up.

SP101: Elementary Spanish I

Performance target: consistent Novice Mid with guided extension toward Novice High
Five units, 24 regular lessons, and five cumulative Cuéntame capstones. This inventory is the control document for what students may be expected to understand and produce at every point in the course.
Reading rule: cultural texts may contain supported receptive language, but student production and grading never exceed the grammar, chunks, vocabulary, and skills already taught. Gerunds enter productively only in L24.

Narrative and cultural route

Mexico City/CEPE and Expo Café → Tepoztlán–Cuernavaca → Mexico City/Xochimilco → Lake Pátzcuaro–Janitzio plus separate monarch territory in Michoacán/State of Mexico → Veracruz/Córdoba. Locations are not interchangeable: each unit names the relevant territory, community, material, period, and source boundary.

Vocabulary control: useful first, culture with support

P — Productive core

Use it in daily life

8–12 high-frequency words or complete chunks needed for the task and recycled in campus life, U.S. interactions, services, or travel.

M — Mexican anchor

Situate the encounter

3–5 words that name a local object, place, food, or practice. They become productive only when they also have immediate communicative value.

R — Receptive richness

Keep cultural depth

Regional, Indigenous, historical, ecological, or craft language appears with image, audio, English support, or microgloss and is not required as unsupported production.

Product route: eBook → Compendio (Key Words / Vocabulary + Úsalo hoy labeled Daily life, Travel, or Both) → Composición → Reto → Cuéntame. Transfer cards appear only after the required structure is available; they do not create a parallel tourist vocabulary bank.

Lesson-by-lesson thematic and linguistic inventory

The “P” column is a planning ceiling, not a vocabulary dump. Each eBook introduces the language in context; the Compendio organizes it; Composición and Reto activate it; Cuéntame integrates it without adding productive grammar.

SP102: Elementary Spanish II

ACTFL Target: Intermediate Low
Narration, obligation, and experience. Learners tell stories about the past, explain habits, describe people in detail, and sustain longer conversations: creating with the language for the first time.

Cluster 1: Shopping & Transactions
Markets & Money		 Shopping: products, prices, markets, bargaining. Numbers 101+. Clothing expanded: llevar, ponerse, quitarse + wardrobe vocabulary. Regular preterite (-é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron). Indirect object pronouns (me, te, le, nos, les). Demonstrative adjectives (este, ese, aquel). Past narrative sequence: primero, luego, después, al final, ese día, la semana pasada. Accent rules: agudas, graves, esdrújulas. Preterite pronunciation: habló (past) vs. hablo (present): the accent mark as grammar signal. Price negotiation: ¿Cuánto cuesta?, ¿Me da un descuento?, Es muy caro. Bargaining culture in Mexican markets.Cultural anchor: Don Marcos selling at the feria: haggling, rapport, trust. Circumlocution: Es una cosa que sirve para... / Es un lugar donde... / Es como un/una...: the key survival strategy for this level.
Cluster 2: Daily Routines & Habits
Personal Care & Culture		 Personal care (despertarse, bañarse, vestirse). Cultural habits. Professions expanded: ¿A qué se dedica? + traits for different jobs. Reflexive verbs (levantarse, acostarse, vestirse). Indefinite & negative words (alguien/nadie, algo/nada).
Uses of "se": Part 1: Reflexive se (La madre se baña) and lexical se: verbs that simply include se in their dictionary form (olvidarse, darse cuenta, morirse).		 Frequency & negation: siempre, a veces, nunca, nadie, nada, tampoco, ni...ni. Sequence: antes de, después de, al + infinitive. Diacritic accent: el/él, tu/tú, si/sí, se/sé. b/v in Mexican Spanish (same sound); c/s/z seseo. Expanded register: adjusting tone by relationship. Indirect preference: me gusta más..., prefiero..., no me gusta tanto.Cultural anchor: Morning routines across Mexican families: la comida as social ritual, sobremesa. Lexical approximation: using a close word when the exact one is unknown (lavar for limpiar). Staying in Spanish instead of switching to English.
Cluster 3: Food, Celebrations & Experiences
Social Life		 Food, meals, gastronomy. Social meals. Celebrations (cumpleaños, quinceañera, fiestas patrias, Día de Muertos). Gustar + faltar, quedar (me falta tiempo, nos quedan dos días). Preterite of SER and IR (fui, fuiste, fue...).
Uses of "se": Part 2: Impersonal se: no specific subject (se puede, se dice, se habla español). Passive se: the thing receives the action (se venden tamales).		 Comparison: más que, menos que, tan...como, mejor, peor. Expansion: que, lo que: building longer sentences. Narrative intonation: continuation vs. closure. Spelling changes in irregular preterites (busqué, llegué, empecé). Food as social act (sobremesa). Celebrations as family cohesion. Mexican indirectness: diminutives to soften (un momentito, cerquita, ahorita). Courtesy expressions quisiera, me gustaría used as fixed phrases (the full conditional verb system comes in SP201).Cultural anchor: Quinceañera planning: doña Rosa organizing the celebration. Paraphrasing: restating ideas to confirm understanding (Entonces, ¿tú dices que...?). Narration management: connectors as crutches (y entonces..., y luego...).
Cluster 4: Health & Wellbeing
Body, Symptoms, Services		 Health, symptoms, medical services (me duele, tengo fiebre, la farmacia). Basic body parts. Preterite of stem-changing verbs. Double object pronouns (se lo dije). Saber vs. conocer. Tener que + infinitive (obligation); soler + infinitive (habits). Contextual framing: antes, mientras, generalmente, de niño/a, en esa época. Spatial reference: este/ese/aquel. Speech rhythm: natural phrasing in longer sentences. Discursive pauses in oral narration. Medical visit in Mexican context (pharmacy first, cultural self-medication). Register: formal with doctor, informal with friend.Cultural anchor: Tomás at the farmacia: navigating health advice without a doctor visit. Emerging self-correction: noticing and correcting own errors in real time (dijo... no, dije).
Cluster 5: Past Narration
Telling Stories		 Biographical vocabulary. Historical events (basic). Narrated experiences & milestones. Irregular preterites (tuve, hice, puse, dije, traje). Verbs that change meaning in preterite (saber = find out, conocer = meet).
Preterite vs. Imperfect contrast: the most important grammar structure at this level. Completed actions vs. habitual or background description. Treated as a dedicated structure, like SER/ESTAR was in SP101.		 Narrative frame: mientras + imperfect background, preterite for main events. Temporal anchoring: en esa época, de niño, antes. Preterite vs. present stress (habló/hablo): now productive, not just receptive. Narration as social act: telling a personal experience, sharing an anecdote: why storytelling matters in interaction.Cultural anchor: Don Felipe narrating his journey to the feria: imperfect for background, preterite for events. Sustaining a brief story without collapsing. Requesting time: A ver..., Es que..., Bueno, lo que pasó fue que...
Cluster 6: Describing & Comparing
People, Objects, Experiences		 People description (physical + personality). Clothing in narrative context. Comparison vocabulary. Comparatives & superlatives. Relative pronouns (que, quien, lo que). Contrast qué/cuál. A personal: now actively produced: Busco a mi hermano vs. Busco un libro. Imperfect (hablaba, comía, vivía). Adverbs. Reciprocal se (preview): se saludan, se abrazan. Expansion: que, lo que, el/la cual: longer sentences with basic subordination. Descriptive sequencing. Full accentuation rules applied. Orthographic preterite changes consolidated. Describing people including clothing: ser for identity, estar for state, llevar for clothing.Cultural anchor: The feria's vendors and visitors: a descriptive scene using all SP102 tools. All SP102 strategies with increasing independence. Circumlocution + approximation as default survival tools.

SP201: Elementary Spanish III

ACTFL Target: Intermediate Mid/High
Subjectivity, future, and hypothesis. Learners express opinions, give commands, react emotionally, debate social issues, and begin exploring hypothetical thinking: using the subjunctive to say what they feel, not just what they know.

Social regulation: commands vary by relationship (boss, friend, parent, stranger). Register: tú/usted + formal/informal commands together.Cultural anchor: Doña Rosa giving instructions for the quinceañera: firm with vendors, warm with family. A narrative that shows how register shifts in real Mexican social contexts. Strategic discourse planning: mentally organizing before speaking. Crutch markers: pues, a ver, lo que quiero decir es...
Cluster 2: Feelings & Reactions
Subjectivity & Emotion		 Wellbeing, exercise & nutrition. Values, cultural identity & social thought. Opinion vocabulary: creer, opinar, considerar, proponer, sugerir, recomendar: verbs that trigger the subjunctive, learned as functional vocabulary. Present subjunctive with verbs of emotion (me alegra que...), doubt (dudo que...) and negation (no creo que...). Subjunctive in adjective clauses (busco un lugar que tenga...). Subjunctive vs. indicative contrast in noun clauses. Cause-emotion-result: me alegra que..., es triste que..., me preocupa que... Concession: aunque + indicative/subjunctive; a pesar de que; si bien: acknowledging the opposing view before responding. Subjunctive spelling: systematic changes (busque, llegue, empiece; conozca, produzca). Discourse connector pronunciation as fluid phonetic blocks. Nuanced opinion: creo que + indicative vs. no creo que + subjunctive: grammar as a tool for expressing stance. Complex courtesy: me gustaría que..., ¿sería posible que...?, ¿te importaría...?Cultural anchor: A community discussion about a local issue: formal register, emotional reactions, respectful disagreement. Historical context: the tradition of collective decision-making in Mexican communities. Advanced paraphrasing: es decir, o sea, en otras palabras: for clarifying both your own and your interlocutor's meaning.
Cluster 3: Future Plans & Possibilities
Projection & Hypothesis		 Environment, nature & conservation. Social issues (recycling, pollution, protected areas, natural resources). Future simple (hablaré, comeré, viviré). Conditional simple (hablaría, comería, viviría). Introduction to imperfect subjunctive in fixed conditional structures: Si tuviera tiempo, viajaría... Si pudiera, lo haría. Debería / sugerir que (recommendation); podría / me gustaría (possibility & courtesy). Temporal projection: más adelante, en el futuro, a largo plazo, para entonces. Condition & consequence: si + present → future; si + imperfect subjunctive → conditional (introduction). Future & conditional accentuation: agudas (hablaré) and graves (hablaría): accent as morphological anchor. Group plan coordination: ¿nos vemos?, te aviso: social commitment vs. literal commitment in Mexican culture. Indirect style: diminutives + conditional to soften requests.Cultural anchor: Planning a community project: negotiating, proposing, projecting consequences. Written text management: plan → write → review. Editing questions: Is it cohesive? Coherent? Is the register appropriate?
Cluster 4: Experience & Reflection
Looking Back, Looking Forward		 Values, identity, cultural thought. Cross-cultural comparison vocabulary. Present perfect (he hablado, has comido, hemos vivido). Nosotros commands (hablemos, comamos). He visto, hemos decidido (accumulated experience); deber + infinitive / haber que (formal regulation). Transition markers: sin embargo, por lo tanto, además, a pesar de, no obstante, por un lado... por otro. Argumentative text organization: en primer lugar / en segundo lugar / finalmente; en conclusión / en resumen. Formal writing: capitalization, complex punctuation (commas in subordinate clauses, semicolons). Extended oral production: rhythm control, strategic pauses, emphasis. Argumentation: evidence + concession (aunque...) + conclusion. Partial disagreement & hedging: Sí, pero... / No sé, pero yo creo que... / Tal vez tengas razón, pero... / Puede ser que... / Lo dudo mucho.Cultural anchor: Debating a social issue: balancing directness with Mexican indirectness norms. Hedging as strategy: memorized formulas for maintaining discussion without confrontation. Active self-correction: conscious monitoring of your own speech.
Cluster 5: Opinion & Argument
Taking a Stance		 Abstract social vocabulary: migration, diversity, social justice, education, technology's role. Advanced relative pronouns (que, quien, cuyo, el/la cual). Full subjunctive vs. indicative contrast in noun clauses: consolidated. Full argumentative structure: thesis → argument → concession → conclusion. All SP201 connectors consolidated. All SP201 pronunciation & writing content consolidated. Fluent production of discourse markers as phonetic blocks. Channel adaptation: text message vs. formal email vs. oral presentation vs. spontaneous conversation: choosing the right linguistic tools for the medium.Cultural anchor: Writing an opinion piece for a community audience: connecting personal cultural experience to broader social argument. All SP201 strategies consolidated with independence. Writing process management as habitual practice.
